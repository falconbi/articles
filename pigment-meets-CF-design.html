Does Pigment meet the design?

Mapping the Forecast Cash Flow & Balance Sheet posting-engine brief against what the Pigment build actually delivers.

PatternPosting engine / sub-ledger
PlatformPigment
FindingEngine met · edges are build effort
01

Verdict

Pigment meets the design. Cash is driven by per-account timing assumptions, those accounts are mapped to CF lines and BS contra accounts, the engine allows module overrides and postings from other applications, and balances accumulate from stored movements with reconciliation checks on top.

The calculation of cash is one engine, driven entirely by the account assumptions. Everything else — mapping, routing, controls — sits around it. The same drivers in produce the same cash out; nothing is reverse-engineered from a blind net-income figure.

02

The shared engine

Per-account timing assumptions spread each account's amount into its cash month, account by account. Those cash movements map to CF lines and to BS contra accounts, accumulate into life-to-date balances, and blend with overrides and imported actuals. In one line:

account timing assumptions→cash by account→mapped to CF lines & BS contras→accumulated into balances→blended with overrides & external postings→checked to zero

Reconciliation sits on top as a control — the check metrics confirm the CF statement ties to the balance-sheet cash movement every period. It verifies the engine; it is not how cash is calculated.

04

Complexity

Highfront-loaded into discovery
Conceptually demanding, mechanically tractable, and dominated by discovery — not by build speed.

The mechanical complexity is moderate and repetitive: one generic engine, a handful of grouped methods covering ~80% of accounts, then per-account parameters. Once the pattern is set, adding an account is cheap. Where the real complexity lives:

  • The edge cases carry disproportionate weight. Deferred revenue running backwards, debt introducing circularity, FX gain/loss as a translation residual — each is its own problem, and none is knowable from the chart of accounts. The expensive part is discovery: the week-six "oh, except for that one."
  • Circularity is the one genuine step-up. It moves from "hard to get right" to "won't compute in a single pass." Interest-on-opening-balance is the correct dodge, but knowing to make that choice, and defending it, is senior judgment — not configuration.
  • The integrity model earns the complexity. Source-module isolation and balance-by-construction are more rules to author, but they turn debugging from an unbounded hunt into a localised lookup — up-front rule proliferation traded against far cheaper lifetime maintenance.

What makes it manageable is that the hard parts are isolated: the 80% flow through grouped methods and stay boring, so the difficulty is quarantined in the bespoke tail rather than smeared across the whole model. That is the design's main achievement — not removing complexity, but containing it.

05

The estimate point

The trade is justified for a recurring, audited, multi-year planning model; harder to justify for a small or short-lived one. A configuration-only estimate prices the build and implicitly assumes requirements are known and data fits — and so misses exactly the discovery-led part that dominates the effort.

Pigment carries the pattern. It does not remove the discovery — the per-account timing assumptions and special-account treatment surface through workshops, not from the chart of accounts.
